Japandi Interior Style: How to Get the Calm, Warm Minimal Look at Home

Japanese calm meets Scandinavian comfort: low furniture, warm wood, earthy colours and handmade pieces. How to create a Japandi room that feels peaceful, not empty.

By Farnaz Sadeghian, certified interior decorator · 6 Oct 2026 · 3 min read

Japandi interior style is a calm blend of Japanese and Scandinavian design. From Japan it takes low furniture, natural materials, darker wood and the beauty of handmade, imperfect things. From Scandinavia it takes comfort, light wood and soft textiles. Together they create rooms that feel peaceful and warm at the same time.

Many of my followers ask me how to make a small apartment feel bigger and quieter. Japandi is often my answer. It is not about buying new things; it is about choosing fewer, better ones. Here is where the style comes from, what defines it, and how to create it at home.

Where Japandi comes from

The word Japandi, a mix of "Japan" and "Scandi", became popular around 2016, but the friendship between the two design cultures is much older. Danish and Finnish designers of the mid-twentieth century admired Japanese craft and simplicity, and both cultures share a respect for natural materials, careful making and a home that is connected to nature.

Two ideas sit behind the style. From Japan comes wabi-sabi, finding beauty in simple, natural and slightly imperfect things, like a hand-thrown bowl or weathered wood. From Denmark comes hygge, the warm feeling of comfort and togetherness. Japandi is where the two meet.

The five elements that define it

1. Low, grounded furniture

Sofas, beds and tables sit lower than usual, which makes ceilings feel higher and rooms more restful. Shapes are simple, with gentle curves and visible legs or solid wooden bases.

2. Warm wood, light and dark together

Japandi often mixes pale oak or ash with darker walnut, smoked oak or black-stained wood. That contrast is what makes it different from pure Scandinavian style.

3. Earthy neutral colours

Walls in warm off-white, clay or soft beige, sometimes with a limewash or plaster texture. Accents in charcoal, black, olive or sage. Nothing bright or shiny.

4. Natural, handmade textures

Linen, cotton, jute, paper, bamboo, rattan, stone and handmade ceramics. A paper lantern, a woven basket and a stoneware vase can do more than any expensive decoration.

5. Space and quiet

Storage is hidden, surfaces are mostly clear, and each object has room around it. One branch in a vase, one piece of art, one beautiful bowl. Emptiness is not missing decoration; it is part of the design.

The Japandi colour palette

Build the room on warm neutrals: rice white, sand, oat, clay and warm grey. Add depth with charcoal, dark walnut and black, and bring life with muted greens from plants or a sage cushion. Avoid cool, blue-whites and very bright colours; Japandi light should feel like late afternoon.

My tip: before you buy anything for a Japandi room, take three things out. The style starts with what you remove.

How to use Japandi style in a modern home

  • Edit the room first: hide cables, remotes and everyday clutter in closed storage.
  • Choose a low sofa or bed in linen or cotton in oat, sand or warm grey.
  • Add one darker wood piece, such as a walnut coffee table or a black-framed bench, to ground the light tones.
  • Use paper or fabric lamps for soft, indirect light; avoid bright spotlights.
  • Pick one natural rug in jute, wool or cotton.
  • Display only a few handmade objects: a ceramic vase, a tea set, a stone bowl.
  • Bring in nature with branches, a bonsai or one large leafy plant.

In Dubai apartments with glossy white floor tiles, a large textured rug and a few wooden pieces will soften the shine. Sheer linen curtains turn strong sunlight into the gentle, diffused light that Japandi loves.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Going too empty. Without texture and warmth, Japandi becomes cold minimalism.
  • Mixing too many wood tones. Choose two, one light and one dark, and repeat them.
  • Fake materials everywhere. A little real wood, linen or ceramic matters more than many plastic look-alikes.
  • Bright, cool lighting. Use warm bulbs and lamps at low heights.

Japandi vs Scandinavian

Scandinavian rooms are brighter, whiter and cosier, with more soft textiles. Japandi rooms are lower, earthier and more restrained, with darker wood accents and fewer objects. Both love natural materials and simplicity, so they mix easily. If you want the lighter version, read my guide to Scandinavian interior style.

A quieter home

Japandi is a style you feel more than see: a room where your eyes can rest. Start with one corner, clear it, add one natural texture and one handmade object, and enjoy the calm. Japandi is one of the fifty styles in my book Soul of the Room, where I show how to bring each style into a real home.

Questions about Japandi style

What is Japandi interior style?

Japandi is a blend of Japanese and Scandinavian design. It combines the Japanese love of simplicity, natural materials and imperfect handmade objects with Scandinavian comfort, light wood and cosy textiles. The result is a calm, uncluttered home with low furniture, earthy neutral colours and warm, indirect light.

Where does the word Japandi come from?

Japandi is a mix of the words Japan and Scandi. The name became popular around 2016, but Japanese and Scandinavian designers have admired each other's work for much longer, because both traditions value craft, natural materials, simplicity and a close connection to nature.

What colours are used in Japandi interiors?

Japandi uses warm, earthy neutrals: off-white, beige, sand, clay, warm grey and soft greige, with deeper accents in charcoal, dark walnut, black and muted sage or olive green. Colours are calm and natural, never bright, so materials and textures can stand out.

How is Japandi different from minimalism?

Japandi is minimal but warm. It keeps rooms uncluttered like minimalism, but adds texture, wood grain, handmade ceramics, linen and plants. It also accepts imperfection, inspired by the Japanese idea of wabi-sabi, so a Japandi room feels lived in and personal rather than strict.

Can I do Japandi on a budget?

Yes. Japandi is about removing things more than buying them. Clear the room, keep a few good pieces, and add low-cost natural textures: a jute rug, linen cushions, a paper lantern, branches in a simple vase and one or two handmade ceramics from a local market.
